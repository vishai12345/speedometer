    About Pacer

    Pacer turns your phone's GPS into a speedometer. It runs in the browser, works offline once loaded, and never sends your location anywhere.

    How speed is measured

    • Doppler speed first. Phones measure speed from the Doppler shift of satellite signals. It is accurate to a few tenths of a km/h and is used whenever the phone provides it.
    • Position fallback. If a phone gives no Doppler speed, Pacer divides the distance between two good fixes (both within 25 m) by the time between them, and marks the signal as weak.
    • Filtering. A small Kalman filter smooths the readings without adding noticeable delay. One wild reading is ignored; two in a row are trusted, so hard braking still shows immediately.
    • Standing still reads 0. GPS drifts by 1–3 km/h when parked. Pacer shows 0 until it has three readings above 3 km/h, or one above 9 km/h.
    • No fake readings. Until the first usable fix, or after 4 s without one, the speed shows "––" instead of 0 or a frozen number.

    Distance and trip time

    • Distance adds up speed × time between GPS fixes. Summing the distance between raw positions overcounts, because the position jitters by several metres even when parked.
    • Short gaps (up to 5 s) are filled from speed. Longer gaps of up to 60 s, such as a tunnel, use the straight line between the fixes on either side. That slightly undercounts curved roads.
    • Elapsed time counts while Pacer is receiving GPS. Moving time counts only while you're above about 2 km/h.

    Validation

    The measurement engine is tested against simulated GPS traces with known true speed and distance. The tests run at 1 reading per second with realistic noise. Results from the current version:

    These numbers are from simulated traces. Real phones vary, so treat Pacer as a driver aid, not a calibrated instrument. Your car's speedometer usually reads a few percent high by design.

    0–100 timing

    The timer arms after you've been stopped for 1.5 s. The start is the moment the car leaves zero, estimated from the first two moving readings. The finish is interpolated between the readings either side of 100 km/h (60 mph in mph). A run is marked rough if the GPS skipped a reading or was imprecise. At one reading per second, expect about ±0.5 s.

    Privacy

    Everything stays on this phone. Settings, the current trip and saved trips are kept in the browser's local storage. Clearing your browser data, or Delete all in Settings, removes them.
